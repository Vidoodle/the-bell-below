# Saint Orra and the Bell

**Status:** Working canon

Orra was the cathedral's high priestess and keeper of the Bell of Mercy. She was declared a saint after the siege; she did not hold that title when the ritual was performed. The drowned cathedral was subsequently renamed for her.

The Bell of Mercy originally served a funerary purpose. During the siege, Orra explained how its rite could be reversed and what that reversal would cost. The council authorized the ritual, the military completed a physical ward around the part of Grayhaven to be protected, and Orra performed the rite. The cathedral was deliberately designated as the place where diverted deaths would fall.

Whenever someone protected by the ward suffered an otherwise fatal injury, a refugee inside the cathedral received that death instead. The victims were selected because they were already gathered beneath the bell and Grayhaven's leaders considered their lives expendable.

To those above, the effect appeared miraculous: mortal wounds closed and fallen defenders rose to fight again. The public account holds that the bell preserved their lives. It does not record where their wounds went.

The ritual did not allow its victims to die normally. Their bodies remain animate within the drowned cathedral: some little more than corpses driven by the curse, others retaining enough memory and will to become wights or revenants. The cathedral's wards contain them below the city.

After the battle, Orra remained below to bind the bell and complete the seal. She died in the cathedral. Grayhaven's public history therefore preserves one edited truth: Orra gave her life performing the miracle that saved the city. It omits everyone whose life she deliberately spent first.

The victims were removed from official history to conceal the crime. That erasure is political, not the cause of their undeath. Recovering their identities can prove what happened or affect individual rites, but freeing the undead does not require reconstructing a complete list of names.

## Unresolved

- The bell's ordinary funerary rite and how Orra learned it could be reversed.
- The exact ward geometry, ritual components, and duration.
- Whether Orra proposed the sacrifice or presented it after the council demanded a way to save the city.
- Why the bell has begun to ring again now.
- What kinds of undead inhabit each part of the cathedral and how much identity they retain.
