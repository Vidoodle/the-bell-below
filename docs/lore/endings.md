# Endings

**Status:** Working canon

The ritual created two connected conditions: the fatal injuries diverted during the siege remain suspended, and the refugees remain bound as undead beneath the cathedral. The endings determine what happens to that unpaid debt and to the people who received it.

## Reseal the bell

The protagonist renews the ritual and keeps the diverted deaths suspended. Living people whose deaths were diverted remain alive, and the undead remain contained within the cathedral.

Grayhaven is preserved without confronting the cost of its survival. This is a delay rather than a permanent solution; the seal will eventually fail again. The exact price of renewing it is unresolved.

## The final toll

If the protagonist fails to intervene, the bell completes its toll. The fatal wound taken from each protected survivor returns, killing anyone still alive. The undead remain trapped in the cathedral, still unable to pass on.

The toll collects Grayhaven's suspended debt without repairing the harm done to the refugees. This is the primary failure ending.

## Free the dead

The protagonist dismantles the binding and releases the undead from the cathedral. The diverted deaths return to every protected survivor who is still alive, just as they do after the final toll. Unlike the failure ending, the refugees are freed from the curse and can finally die.

This is a deliberate but morally costly resolution. Seren was protected by the ritual during the siege and will die if he chooses this ending.

## Unresolved

- Whether the adventure needs a third successful ending in addition to resealing and freeing the dead.
- What renewing the seal costs and whether it requires another sacrifice.
- What actions and discoveries make freeing the dead possible.
- Whether any protected survivor can willingly take or redistribute another person's returned death.
- How the ending changes when the protagonist is Seren.
