# The Siege

**Status:** Working canon

Forty years ago, several failed harvests turned the customary seasonal raids into something much larger. The clans combined their fleets, burned farms and villages across the delta, and drove the survivors behind Grayhaven's walls. Instead of withdrawing with what they could carry, the raiders remained, blockaded the river mouth, and attempted to seize the city's stores before winter.

The siege reached its crisis at low water, when the raiders broke through a tide gate and entered the Lower Ward along the exposed channel. When the tide returned, the damaged gate could not be closed and the incoming water poured into the district behind them. Grayhaven's defenders were depleted, fires were spreading, and the granaries were about to be taken. The city was hours from losing the battle that would determine who survived the winter.

The cathedral that now bears Saint Orra's name stood in the Lower Ward and had become a shelter for refugees from the delta. Grayhaven's civic, military, and religious leaders deliberately chose to perform the bell ritual. Fatal wounds suffered within the part of the city enclosed by the protective ward were transferred to the refugees inside the cathedral. Grayhaven's defenders survived the final assault and drove the raiders out.

Grayhaven declared that the refugees had died in the flood. The city rebuilt its defenses farther inland, abandoned the inundated district, and sealed the cathedral beneath the water rather than recover the dead or expose what had been done.

The official history remembers the event as Saint Orra's miracle. The refugees' sacrifice was erased from the record.

## Thematic foundation

The raiders attacked because their people were starving. Grayhaven sacrificed the refugees because its leaders believed their own city would otherwise be destroyed. Neither act becomes innocent because it was committed for survival.
